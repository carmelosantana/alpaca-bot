# What people actually ask for from AI plugins in WordPress — and what would make Alpaca Bot worth using in 2026

Research for Kanboard #2958. Compiled 2026-09-05. Install/rating figures pulled from `api.wordpress.org/plugins/info/1.2/` on 2026-09-05; WordPress core current version confirmed as 7.1 via `api.wordpress.org/core/version-check/1.7/`. Roughly 35 individual WordPress.org support threads and reviews were opened and read in full, plus the WordPress AI team's Make blog, the WordPress/ai and WordPress/mcp-adapter repositories, and named ecosystem commentary.

## TL;DR

1. **Self-hosting is no longer a differentiator, and the "local LLM" niche is nearly empty.** AI Engine (100k installs), AI Puffer (10k) and MxChat (2k) all ship Ollama/llama.cpp/LM Studio support, and the WordPress AI team already blessed Fueled's *AI Provider for Ollama* as a featured community connector. Every dedicated local-model connector in the directory has **under 300 active installs**. Alpaca Bot cannot win by being a better Ollama plugin.
2. **The loudest asks are governance, not capability.** Consent (SiteGround's bundled AI Agent: ~1,000,000 installs, **32/100 rating, 81 of 96 ratings 1-star**, reviews almost entirely about non-consensual installation) and cost (surprise bills, token burn users didn't cause, no server-side spend caps) dominate every forum read.
3. **The single largest volume of unmet need in the category is MCP transport plumbing** — roughly **35 of 60 threads** across Royal MCP and Easy MCP AI are OAuth/Dynamic-Client-Registration/connector failures, usually caused by hosts, caches and WAFs rather than the plugin. That is a warning about what building an MCP server costs you in support.
4. **Users want AI to write to their site and are simultaneously frightened of it** — the strongest artifact found is a user-authored PSA after MCP meta-writes double-serialized and corrupted his data; Elementor's agentic *Angie* sits at 3.2/5 with reviews calling it "not yet ready for production".
5. **Alpaca Bot is dormant:** 20 active installs, 1 review, last released 2024-07-06, tested to 6.5.10 against a current core of **7.1**, carrying the directory's "hasn't been tested with the latest 3 major releases" banner. Its defensible 1.0 is **local-first grounding plus governance** — cost caps, receipts, safe writes — not another chat window.

---

## Method, and what this evidence can and cannot support

- **Alpaca Bot has almost no primary user signal of its own**: one review, one WordPress.org support thread, one externally-filed GitHub issue. Everything about "what users want" is therefore market evidence from comparable plugins, not Alpaca Bot user evidence.
- **Reddit could not be used** — `reddit.com` blocks this crawler, so r/Wordpress is unrepresented. That is a real gap in the community layer.
- **WP Tavern turned out to be a weak source.** Its `/category/plugins` newest entries are from December 2024 and its 2026 `/tag/ai` archive is entirely podcast episodes; the "AI and WordPress" forum has three topics total. Its AI coverage is now interview-led, not news-led.
- **Ratings** are WordPress.org's 0–100 scale (98 = 4.9 stars). **Install counts are bucketed** ("100,000+"), so they are floors.
- **Counts of the form "18 of 30 threads"** are counts of unambiguous thread titles on a forum listing page, not a sampled estimate.
- Claims that could not be substantiated are listed explicitly in *Negative findings* below rather than quietly dropped. No figures here are estimated or invented.

## Part 1 — Ranked asks and pain points

### 1. Consent, permission, and an off-switch

**The strongest evidence base in the entire study.** SiteGround pushed its AI Agent onto customer sites pre-activated and pre-configured as the default AI connector, and was punished for it.

| Metric (2026-09-05) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Active installs | 1,000,000+ |
| Rating | **32/100** |
| 1-star ratings | **81 of 96** |

Review titles are about the *installation method*, not the AI: *"Installed without consent"*, *"The AI Squatter – Moved in without permission"*, *"Please Don't Install Stuff on My Website Without My Permission!"*, *"This plugin fits the definition of Malware and should be blacklisted"*, *"Installed without consent on 200+ client websites"*, *"Like a U2 Album on my iPhone"* ([reviews](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/sg-ai-studio/reviews/?filter=1)). One reviewer: *"this plugin should have never been forced on anyone's site"*; SiteGround replied that it emailed in advance and that *"the plugin is fully passive until used and it can be deactivated at any time"* — which did not address the objection ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/unprofessional-forced-install/)). WP Mayor tracked the collapse: ~1.1 stars within days (35 of 36 reviews at one star), 1.3 stars with 59 of 64 at one star by 2026-06-11 ([WP Mayor](https://wpmayor.com/siteground-ai-plugin-controversy/)).

The whole ecosystem is now spending engineering effort here:

- Official AI plugin 1.3.0 made custom abilities **opt-in**: `ai/get-post-details`, `ai/get-post-terms`, `core/read-settings`, `core/read-users`, `core/read-content` are *"no longer registered automatically"* ([What's new in AI 1.3.0](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/08/18/whats-new-in-ai-1-3-0/)).
- It also ships **"Connector Approvals – Require explicit administrator approval before plugins or themes can use AI connectors configured on this site"** ([plugin page](https://wordpress.org/plugins/ai/)).
- An entire plugin exists to gate AI: **Enable Abilities for MCP** (2,000+ installs) — *"full control over which WordPress Abilities are available to AI assistants"*, 101 abilities across 21 categories, per-ability toggles, role enforcement ([plugin page](https://wordpress.org/plugins/enable-abilities-for-mcp/)).
- Marcus Burnette: **"I don't want magic here. I want receipts"** and **"You can't centralize the convenience and then decentralize the consequences"** — asking for a permission layer that can enable AI in wp-admin but disable it on the front end, set budget thresholds, and block specific plugins ([The WP World, 2026-03-24](https://thewp.world/wordpress-ai-connectors-need-more-friction-not-less/)).
- Role gating is an open user request on the official plugin: *"Limit use to certain roles?"* ([forum](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/ai/)).

### 2. MCP transport reliability — the largest volume of unmet need

Not an AI problem at all; a plumbing problem. Counting unambiguous thread titles on the forum listings:

- **Royal MCP: 18 of 30 threads** are OAuth / Dynamic Client Registration / connector failures.
- **Easy MCP AI: ~17 of 30**, same shape, plus tool-schema validation bugs.
- **AI Engine:** at least 5 (*"Claude.ai and AI Engine MCP OAuth issues"*, *"MCP OAuth: Claude.ai connector fails at Dynamic Client Registration"*, *"MCP Server SSE connection hangs with no API key configured"*, and a 35-reply Claude Desktop capabilities thread).

The best-documented case ran 16 replies over weeks:

> "OAuth authorization fails when connecting to Claude.ai… We believe Claude.ai first sends a GET request to verify the server is reachable. Since it gets a **405**, it considers the server unreachable and never starts the OAuth flow." … **"The requests from Claude.ai never reach WordPress PHP during the OAuth handshake."**
> — terraexperty, [Royal MCP](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/siteground-wordpress-claude/)

Root causes he isolated: static files served with the wrong Content-Type, **SiteGround's nginx intercepting `/.well-known/*`**, and **Dynamic Cache poisoning the OAuth endpoints**. Blockers named across these threads: SiteGround, LiteSpeed, Cloudflare, Xneelo, ModSecurity, Avast, `python-httpx` User-Agent 403s, subdirectory installs, unflushed permalinks.

Second failure mode — connects, exposes nothing: OAuth authorizes but *"exposes ZERO callable tools to the AI session"* while the same endpoint returns **all 213 tools** with an API token ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/claude-cant-see-any-abilities/)). Third — schema incompatibility across clients: *"invalid input_schema – Can't Use Plugin"*, *"Tool Schemas Incompatible with Gemini Function Calling API"*, *"Royal MCP 1.4.39 returns invalid JSON-RPC version '2'"*, *"Claude.ai connector fails — 'SSE streaming not supported'"*.

Both Royal MCP and Easy MCP AI maintain **pinned staff triage threads** (*"Connection Issue — Read This First"*, *"Royal MCP Troubleshooting — Read First"*), which is itself evidence of volume.

**Read this as a cost warning.** MCP forum volume dwarfs the local-model theme by roughly **25:1** in this sample — the category is hot, and its support burden is brutal and mostly not the plugin author's fault.

### 3. Cost, token spend and surprise bills

Felt as *betrayal*, not as a line item. The wound is surprise, and spend the owner didn't cause.

**Free plugin, someone else's bill:**
> "Not for the beginner. Run up costs without knowing as the plugin is free but won't work without a valid OpenAI subscription." … "Constantly saying used up quota…even from the beginning when I only said 'Hi'…"
> — pauldflynn, 1★ AI Engine, [*"Too complex. Hidden charges from OpenAI"*](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/too-complex-hidden-charges-from-openai/). The author's reply — *"it's like reviewing a car based on the fuel price"* — is exactly the gap Alpaca Bot can close, because a local model has no fuel price.

A Portuguese 1★ tells the same story: *"nunca usei o chat gpt… Dá erro dizendo que já usei toda minha cota"* ("never used ChatGPT… it says I've already used my entire quota") ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/plugin-com-problemas-de-cota-excedida/)). An MxChat user hit it on embeddings at *$0.18 / $120.00* usage and got **zero replies** ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/openai-embedding-api-quota-error-although-api-key-is-valid-and-usage-is-0-18/)).

**Tokens drained by traffic the owner didn't cause:**
> "had to stop using this plugin because of all the tokens were eaten in few days by fake empty requests." … "My API was added to your plugin settings. And I lost the tokens for nothing."
> — veris18, 1★ AI Engine ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/cool-plugin-574/)) — bot spam hitting a public chatbot endpoint.
> "the bot sends chat summaries to OpenAI four times daily, rapidly exhausting tokens" — Pat, 1★ WPBot ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/dont-use-it-if-you-want-to-save-your-time/)).

**Token burn from badly shaped tool payloads — the most actionable finding in the sweep:**
> "the `wp_get_page` tool requires Claude to contextualize the entire page even if I want to know a small bit of data about the site, **which quickly burns through tokens**"
> — xanderhbsg, Royal MCP. He shipped working PHP for a minimal read-only endpoint (id, slug, title, permalink, snippet); the vendor agreed — *"Great idea and great point on token usage"* — and shipped `wp_search` with `snippet` and `per_page` in 1.4.32 ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/request-for-new-plugin-capability/)).

**Caps that aren't enforced server-side:** AI Engine's *"Chat remains active after usage limit is reached"* — *"The chat input remains enabled, so users can continue typing"* — was client-side only until 3.6.3, and the earlier fix *"only applied to non-streaming chatbots"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/chat-remains-active-after-usage-limit-is-reached/)).

**Being locked out of your own key:**
> "Does not allow you to use your own API key, you are locked into their expensive marked up generation costs."
> — Brad/saskso, 1★ GetGenie ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/does-not-allow-own-api-key/)). The vendor refused and asked him to remove the review.

Commentary agrees. Russell Aaron: **"Your end user doesn't prompt like that"** and **"There's no graceful failure state built into most of these integrations"** ([russellenvy.com](https://russellenvy.com/wordpress-ai-plugins-token-cost/)). Vapvarun lists **soft budget enforcement** as a con of the 7.0 Connectors API and asks for **"Hard budget limit options"** ([vapvarun.com, 2026-03-30](https://vapvarun.com/wordpress-7-ai-connectors-pros-cons/)). The AI team frames local models the same way: Ollama gives you *"no API key, no cloud, no cost"* ([Call for Testing](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/03/25/call-for-testing-community-ai-connector-plugins/)).

### 4. Write access: wanted badly, feared correctly

**The appetite is explicit.** A 35-reply AI Engine thread asks whether Claude can do *"full page design and layout creation"* in Gutenberg, *"control other plugins and operation in WordPress"*, change theme colors, upload images, execute PHP ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/clarification-on-ai-engine-mcp-capabilities-with-claude-desktop/)). Others want bulk WooCommerce — *"Is there a way to schedule a bulk update on 1000 or 2000+ products all at once?"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/possible-to-update-all-2700-products/)) — and theme code access.

**And the fear is justified.** The single strongest artifact found is a user-authored public PSA:

> **"if you used this feature and specific types of data, you have corrupted data."** — Dave Hilditch
> Author's root cause: *"The MCP post tools called `maybe_serialize()` before `update_post_meta()`, which serializes arrays itself, so array meta got stored double-serialized."*
> [PSA thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/psa-if-you-used-the-mcp-meta-feature-check-your-data/)

The current state of the art in guardrails is simply refusal. Royal MCP declined PHP file writes: *"Adding PHP file write turns that into 'someone plants a backdoor in `functions.php` and hides their tracks.'"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/feature-request-access-to-functions-php-and-others/)). The MCP Adapter's safety story is entirely capability checks plus operator discipline — *"Use `permission_callback` carefully"*, *"Avoid `__return_true` for destructive operations"*, prefer *"read-only abilities for public MCP endpoints"* ([developer.wordpress.org, Feb 2026](https://developer.wordpress.org/news/2026/02/from-abilities-to-ai-agents-introducing-the-wordpress-mcp-adapter/)). **Nobody ships dry-run, diff, or undo.**

Market reception of agentic AI confirms the caution: Elementor's **Angie** (free, MCP-based, sandboxes generated code) sits at **3.2/5 across 17 reviews** — 8 five-star, 3 two-star, 5 one-star, with titles including *"Yea not yet ready for production"*, *"VERY BETA"*, *"I was not able to do anything useful"* ([reviews](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/angie/reviews/)).

### 5. Site-grounded answers (RAG) — and where they actually fail

Every serious competitor leads with RAG: MxChat *"Train your AI chatbot on your website content, WooCommerce products, PDFs, YouTube videos"*; AI Puffer *"Train on Your Data"*; AI Engine gates embeddings, RAG and vector DBs (Pinecone/Chroma/Qdrant) behind Pro; Rapls sells *"RAG hybrid search"* plus a **"Grounded Answers Only"** mode that replies *"I couldn't find that"* instead of hallucinating.

**But ingestion is where it dies, not retrieval** — and the cause is the site's own infrastructure:

> "I was trying to import a sitemap … but got a 403 error. … We are using Cloudflare managed rule to protect our site" — tomchen29
> "PDFs hosted on the SiteGround WordPress site return a 403 … the requests are being blocked by ModSecurity because the MXChat ingestion bot appears to be using an outdated browser-style User-Agent … Their suggested fix was for MXChat to use a custom crawler User-Agent" — mwbcd
> [MxChat thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/knowledge-sitemap-import-doesnt-work/) (fixed in 3.2.13 with a versioned UA, filterable via `mxchat_ingest_user_agent`)

There are **at least 7 knowledge-base ingestion threads** on MxChat's listing alone. Users also want non-WordPress sources (SharePoint), per-conversation document upload, and **role-scoped** knowledge — *"I want to restrict Pages … from certain role types"*, **0 replies, unresolved** ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/restrict-a-page-not-only-a-post-from-knowledge-base/)).

Core is heading the same way: the PHP AI Client's embedding support is *"progressing toward a 1.5.0 release"* — currently *"a schema change… blocks the remaining work"* — and will land in the WordPress AI Client **during the 7.2 cycle**, with embedding storage designed for *the plugin, not core DB tables*. The AI team notes **"Local models, e.g. Ollama, are particularly useful for testing this work"** ([weekly summaries](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/09/04/ai-contributor-weekly-summary-2-september-2026/), [19 Aug](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/08/20/ai-contributor-weekly-summary-19-august-2026/)). Native vector search and C2PA provenance are on the 1.4.0+ roadmap ([AI 1.3.0](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/08/18/whats-new-in-ai-1-3-0/)).

### 6. Setup friction and opaque errors

- Best single verbatim, title included: **"Stupid blocking error with no explanation where to solve"** — *"Failed Plugin database tables present — Missing: easy_mcp_ai_tokens, easy_mcp_ai_audit_log…"*. The real cause was **the MySQL user lacking CREATE privileges**, invisible from inside WordPress ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/stupid-blocking-error-with-no-explanation-where-to-solve/)).
- What earns AI Engine its 98/864 is ease and support, not features: *"Easy to use highly customizable plugin"*, *"Very simple"*, *"Works so well. Amazing documentation"*, *"The best MCP implementation, and the best support from any author"* ([5-star](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/ai-engine/reviews/?filter=5)). Its 1-star counterpart is *"Too complex."*
- The official plugin has an open issue titled *"Improving onboarding"* (#994) and confused threads: *"How does the plugin choose which connector to use?"*, *"Question about the AI plugin and the connector plugin"* ([forum](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/ai/)).
- The AI team recorded the same gap at WordCamp US: attendees **mistook the WordPress AI booth for a vendor table**, and the two named problems were a *"lack of beginner-friendly resources"* and unclear messaging that *"AI features ship via a separate plugin rather than WordPress core."* Crucially, **connector capability discovery is broken and unowned**: *"Users discover unsupported features only after encountering errors"* — the team wants to *"standardize capability advertising"* but agreed **no concrete next steps** ([26 Aug summary](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/08/27/ai-contributor-weekly-summary-26-august-2026/)).
- Rapls shows the pattern to copy: a guided *"Start for free"* panel that *"hands you a free key from OpenRouter or the Google Gemini free tier, tests it, auto-selects a working model, and switches the chatbot on — no AI or API experience needed."*
- A concrete local-model footgun Alpaca Bot will hit: `wp_ai_client_prompt()` at default `init` priority 10 fails silently because the provider is registered but not yet authenticated — use priority 25 ([juanma.codes, 2026-03-29](https://juanma.codes/2026/03/29/using-local-ai-models-with-wordpress-7-0-what-i-learned-connecting-ollama/)). Fueled's connector also had to add configurable timeouts for slow/remote instances ([issue #89](https://github.com/fueled/ai-provider-for-ollama/issues)).

### 7. Local / self-hosted models — real demand, honestly weak

Demand is documented and officially endorsed:

- Official AI plugin thread **"Why exclude Ollama (local) in default package?"** The author explained Anthropic/Google/OpenAI are *"set there by WordPress core, not by the AI plugin"*; the user was disappointed Ollama isn't *"included in WordPress core like Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/why-exclude-ollama-local-in-default-package/)).
- AI Engine thread asking for llama.cpp: *"Since I have found llama.cpp which is more faster, I hope to connect my AI Engine Pro to llama.cpp… And many other LLM loaders or LLMs can support the OpenAI API format."* The answer names the whole competitive set — *"llama.cpp, vLLM, LM Studio, LocalAI, TGI in OpenAI mode, and most small hosted providers all work"* via a Custom (OpenAI-Compatible) connector shipped in 3.4.8 ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/can-ai-engine-support-llama-cpp-or-other-similar-openai-api-compatible-tools/)).
- The AI team: Ollama *"lets you run open-source AI models locally on your own hardware — no API key, no cloud, no cost. Perfect for privacy-conscious sites."* ([Call for Testing](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/03/25/call-for-testing-community-ai-connector-plugins/)).
- Vapvarun lists **"Local AI disadvantage — cloud providers receive preferential treatment in setup and documentation"** as a con and asks for *"Prioritized open-source AI support."*

**Two corrections that matter more than the demand.** First, the one detailed local-model request in the whole forum sweep was framed on **speed and experimentation, not privacy** — and MCP thread volume outweighs local-model threads by roughly 25:1. Second, nobody installs these:

| Local/self-hosted connector | Installs |
| --- | --- |
| [AI Provider for Ollama](https://wordpress.org/plugins/ai-provider-for-ollama/) (Fueled) | 200+ |
| [Ultimate AI Connector for Compatible Endpoints](https://wordpress.org/plugins/ultimate-ai-connector-compatible-endpoints/) | 100+ |
| [Aiify](https://wordpress.org/plugins/aiify/) | 100+ |
| [Axtolab AI Connector](https://wordpress.org/plugins/axtolab-ai-connector/) | 30+ |
| [EasyIT AI Chat](https://wordpress.org/plugins/easyit-ai-chat/) | 20+ |
| [MW Local AI Connector](https://wordpress.org/plugins/mw-local-ai-connector/) (Matt Wiebe) | 10+ |
| [AI Provider for llama.cpp](https://wordpress.org/plugins/ai-provider-for-llamacpp/) | fewer than 10 |
| [Roddyka Local AI Connector for Ollama](https://wordpress.org/plugins/ai-provider-for-ollama-local/) | fewer than 10 |
| **Alpaca Bot** | **20+** |

Meanwhile the plugins treating local as *one option among many* have 100k, 80k and 10k installs. **People want the privacy story; they buy the capability story.**

### 8. Performance, resource use and conflicts

- Assets loaded site-wide when idle: *"MxChat frontend assets loading globally. These are not needed on pages where the chatbot is not inserted"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/mxchat-frontend-assets-are-enqueued-site-wide/)).
- Measured hit with nothing running: *"My website loads with extra 4-5 seconds when AI Puffer enabled, and no task running with it. As soon as I disable it, all comes to normal, website load under 1 second."* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/5-seconds-increased-website-load-time-with-ai-puffer/)).
- Memory blowups: *"en el hosting consume mucha memoria y esto hace tener problemas y te tira la web"* ("consumes a lot of memory… and crashes your website") — 1★, **0 replies** ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/mejorar-recursos-hosting/)).
- 300s timeouts on writes over MCP: *"On a large page with shortcodes that each run a `WP_Query`, that render is what stalls"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/wp_update_page-via-mcp-times-out-300s-on-large-content-reads-create/)).
- Admin crash: *"vibe-ai crashes wp-admin (500/502) for logged-in administrators only"*, workaround *"keep vibe-ai deactivated by default so wp-admin stays usable"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/recurring-wp-admin-crash-for-logged-in-admins/)).
- Official AI plugin: *"Performance regression: AI/Type-Ahead feature forces loading of heavy editor dependencies"* ([forum](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/ai/)).
- Conflicts named across listings: Wordfence, WPBakery, Divi, Elementor, Jetpack, Yoast, WPCode, Varnish, Redis, Autoptimize, Cloudflare, LiteSpeed.

### 9. Privacy, GDPR and PII — an integrator concern, not an end-user one

- Cookies before consent, breaking caching: *"Would it be possible for this plugin to only set the session id cookie ('mwai_session_id') after GDPR consent has been given? This would also help with varnish caching compatibility"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/only-set-mwai_session_id-when-gdpr-consent-given/), fixed 3.6.4).
- PII through MCP tools: Royal MCP *removed* `user_email`/`user_login` from user tools in 1.2.3 *"to reduce the PII surface accessible via MCP"* and is weighing opt-in re-exposure with no timeline ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/safely-exposing-user-email-for-role-to-crm-mapping/)).
- Role-scoped knowledge bases (see §5), and objections to handing admin credentials to support.

Notably, **no end user in this sweep invoked GDPR against a hosted API**. The privacy concern is expressed by developers and integrators, in structural terms.

### 10. API key security

- Patchstack founder Oliver Sild on WordPress 7.0's launch: **"WordPress 7.0 combined with plugin vulnerabilities = free AI tokens. There will be an absolute rush by hackers to steal API keys."** A 7.0 bug also let keys surface in browser autofill in plain text ([Search Engine Journal, 2026-05-22](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/wordpress-7-0-faces-security-concerns-over-ai-api-keys/575679/)).
- A commenter on the Connectors API announcement noted that once a key is registered *"every plugin can access it"* — site-wide, no documented granular control ([Make/Core, 2026-03-18](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2026/03/18/introducing-the-connectors-api-in-wordpress-7-0/)).
- Core's answer: the AI plugin ships *"Key Encryption – Encrypts AI provider API keys at rest using bundled libsodium"*, and the **Secrets API is moving into WordPress 7.2 core** with a requirement for *"a straightforward way for plugins to detect availability of the feature so existing plaintext-secret code can be migrated cleanly"* ([26 Aug summary](https://make.wordpress.org/ai/2026/08/27/ai-contributor-weekly-summary-26-august-2026/)).
- AI Engine itself carries an [RCE](https://patchstack.com/database/wordpress/plugin/ai-engine/vulnerability/wordpress-ai-engine-plugin-2-5-1-admin-rce-vulnerability) and an [SQLi](https://patchstack.com/database/wordpress/plugin/ai-engine/vulnerability/wordpress-ai-engine-plugin-2-4-8-admin-sqli-vulnerability) in Patchstack; a 1-star review reads *"API leaks make no sense using this plugin, despite all the helpful features"*. WPBot drew *"The plugin had SQL Injection vulnerabilities that would allow admin access without any account."*

**This is the strongest structural argument for local-first: with Ollama there is no key to steal and no bill to run up.** That argument is worth far more than the feature "supports Ollama".

### 11. Support responsiveness — sharply bimodal, and exploitable

**Responsive** (AI Engine, MxChat, Royal MCP, Easy MCP AI, WPVibe): authors reply within days and cite shipped version numbers in-thread — 3.6.3, 3.6.4, 3.2.13, 1.4.32, 1.7.6, 0.5.2. AI Engine's 98/864 is largely this.

**Abandoned or hostile** (WPBot, GetGenie, AI Puffer):
- *"Literally they created a plugin, that has issues and they do not bother to even fix them. If you're planning to use it long term, please do not."* — 1★ WPBot, **0 replies** ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/best-1256/)).
- *"It is NOT part of a Software Release cycle to use paying users as testers."* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/recommend-you-avoid/)).
- *"I need every month to accept a new subcsription. If you have questions, directly deep into the plugin the support dont answer anymore."* — 1★ AI Puffer ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/prices-increases-every-month-but-support-0/)).
- **Review-pressure pattern:** in **3 of 3** GetGenie 1-star threads read, the vendor's reply asks the user to remove or revise the rating rather than diagnosing.

### 12. What agencies say they want next — maintenance, not content

**State of WordPress Agencies 2026** (CloudLinux + WebPros), **210 WordPress agencies and freelancers, surveyed 2026-04-14 to 2026-05-15** ([WebPros](https://www.webpros.com/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-state-of-wordpress-agencies-report/), [CloudLinux](https://cloudlinux.com/resources/state-of-wordpress-agencies-2026/)):

- *"Nine in ten agencies are already using AI tools somewhere in their workflow"*; content writing 59%, code generation/debugging 53%, SEO research 39%, site monitoring/maintenance automation 16%.
- **"39% of agencies are already running AI agents"** including Cursor, Claude Code and Windsurf.
- **What they most want from AI next: automated WordPress updates with intelligent pre-update testing (38%) and automated security across client sites (35%)** — both *maintenance*, not generation.
- 63% name AI and automation as the biggest force reshaping the segment; only 37% feel prepared. 78% are solo or ≤10 people; 18% manage 100+ client sites.

### 13. Provenance and disclosure — emerging

WP Tavern #218, Luke Carbis (2026-05-27): proposes a **voluntary plugin header with five AI-disclosure levels**, from "AI used only for idea generation" to "entire plugin written by AI", surfaced in the directory alongside reviews. *"If I want to opt out of AI, I don't have that option if I also want to be a WordPress contributor."* ([episode](https://wptavern.com/podcast/218-luke-carbis-on-the-future-of-wordpress-plugins-ai-ethics-and-new-directory-standards)). ### Negative findings — stated plainly so they aren't re-litigated

- **No substantiated database-bloat complaint.** The AI Engine thread literally titled *"fills database"* turned out to be WooCommerce `_stock` meta; the author corrected it ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/fills-database/)). The *perception* exists; the evidence didn't.
- **No model-deprecation breakage found.** Multi-provider demand is real (Grok requests, OpenRouter suggestions), but no thread reported a deprecated model breaking a site.
- **No end-user GDPR complaint** against a hosted API; that concern is integrator-side only.
- **"AI Workspace" is a draft, not the published roadmap.** The WordPress/ai README's roadmap names **AI Playground, Content Assistant, Site Agent, Workflow Automation** ([repo](https://github.com/WordPress/ai), [board](https://github.com/orgs/WordPress/projects/240/views/1)). Separately, draft issue [#1004 "Add the Global AI Workspace"](https://github.com/WordPress/ai/issues/1004) proposes *"a full-screen conversational admin screen where a site owner talks to an AI that can read their content — under their own capabilities, never above them"*, with `ai/search-content` and `ai/read-content-bodies` read abilities, streaming, and propose-then-confirm writes. Treat it as directional intent, not a shipped commitment. (AI Engine also markets a separate product it calls "Workspace" — don't conflate them.)
- **Unverified statistics deliberately excluded:** "87% of developers concerned about agent accuracy", "81% about security/privacy of agent data", and "trust in AI output collapsed to 29%". These had no attributable source and are contradicted by the primary [State of AI 2026 survey](https://www.d4b.dev/blog/2026-05-23-what-the-state-of-ai-2026-survey-says-about-developer-work) (7,258 responses), which reports hallucinations/inaccuracies at ~64%. That survey is general developers, not WordPress-specific.

## Part 3 — Where Alpaca Bot actually stands

Observed facts:

- **20+ active installs, 1 review** (*"exactly what's needed."*, linussextips, ~2 years 4 months ago), **0 open support threads**, one closed thread, one externally-filed GitHub issue ([#62](https://github.com/carmelosantana/alpaca-bot/issues/62)) — and both the thread and the issue are **the same defect**: passing a boolean where Ollama expects a string when `model` is omitted from the shortcode.
- **Last release 0.4.17 on 2024-07-06**; tested to 6.5.10 against current core **7.1**; the plugin page carries *"This plugin hasn't been tested with the latest 3 major releases of WordPress. It may no longer be maintained or supported…"*
- **Discoverability is degrading.** A directory API search for `ollama` returns 24 results including eight Ollama-related plugins — Alpaca Bot is **not among them**. It is only findable by name.
- **Architecture is Ollama-locked.** `src/Api/Ollama.php` is a bespoke HTTP client; there is no PHP AI Client or Connectors API integration. Worth keeping: `src/Agents/*` (agent interface + registry), `src/Log/Post.php` (request logging), `src/Chat/Screen.php`, the htmx UI, PHP 8.1 baseline. Total `src/` is ~153K — small enough to re-plumb.
- **The only real user request on record was a front-end chat, and it was declined by design**: the author replied it *"was never intended to live on the front-end"* ([thread](https://wordpress.org/support/topic/error-when-using-alpacabot/)).

**The strategic squeeze:** core's published roadmap includes a **Site Agent** ("natural language WordPress administration") and **Content Assistant**, and draft issue #1004 proposes a full-screen admin chat that reads site content under the user's own capabilities. "Chat with your LLM in wp-admin" is on a path to being a core feature.

## Part 4 — Implications for Alpaca Bot 1.0

The defensible position is **not** "Ollama in WordPress" — commoditized, and officially served by Fueled's connector. It is: **the WordPress AI plugin whose marginal cost is zero, whose data never leaves the building, and which can prove both.** That maps directly onto the two loudest asks (governance §1, cost §3) and onto the two failure classes a local-first plugin structurally avoids (key theft §10, WAF-blocked ingestion §5).

### The 5 things to build first

1. **Compatibility and trust reset — blocking; nothing else counts until this ships.**
   Release against WordPress 7.1, clear the "not tested with the latest 3 major releases" banner, restore a visible cadence. Fold in the `model`-attribute type bug that produced *both* recorded user reports — it is the only known user-facing defect and it has bitten two people. Note the plugin is already invisible in `ollama` directory search; a fresh, well-tagged release is the cheapest discoverability fix available.

2. **Re-plumb onto the WordPress AI Building Blocks instead of a bespoke Ollama client.**
   Replace `src/Api/Ollama.php` with the PHP AI Client / Connectors API, and register Alpaca Bot's features as **Abilities**. Ship Ollama as the opinionated default path (recommending Fueled's featured connector rather than competing with it), but stop being provider-locked. Concrete gotchas already documented by others: register at `init` **priority 25**, not 10, or `wp_ai_client_prompt()` fails silently against a registered-but-unauthenticated provider; and expose a filter for Ollama request/connect timeouts. Time this against core: **Abilities CRUD (`core/content-query`, `core/content-update`), the Secrets API, and AI Client embeddings all target the 7.2 cycle** — build to those shapes now.

3. **Locally-grounded answers, with a "grounded only" mode — and server-side ingestion.**
   This is the biggest capability gap versus every competitor, and Alpaca Bot has a structural advantage nobody else has: **it can index the site from inside PHP and never make an outbound HTTP request to itself.** That sidesteps the entire class of failures that kills competitors' RAG — Cloudflare 403s, ModSecurity blocking the crawler UA, PDFs behind the host WAF (§5). Index posts/pages/CPTs with Ollama embeddings, retrieve hybrid keyword+semantic, cite sources, and answer *"I couldn't find that"* rather than inventing. Filter retrieval by the reading user's capabilities — core's phrasing, *"under their own capabilities, never above them"*, is the right bar, and role-scoped knowledge is an unanswered request on MxChat today.

4. **Governance and receipts — the differentiator nobody has shipped.**
   Build what Burnette asked for and what vapvarun says the ecosystem lacks: **server-enforced** hard caps per role/user (requests, tokens, wall-clock, concurrency) — AI Engine's caps were client-side only until 3.6.3 and initially missed streaming, so enforce in PHP on every path; a request log with real attribution (user, feature, model, duration, tokens) built on the existing `src/Log/Post.php`; a **legible** failure state when a cap trips; and per-feature on/off toggles that default to off. Design tool payloads to be snippet-shaped from day one — returning whole pages when a title was asked for is the documented token-burn bug (§3). With a local model the receipts add up to zero, which is a pitch nobody else can make honestly.

5. **Safe writes: propose → diff → confirm → revision-backed undo.**
   Rebuild `[alpacabot_agent]` on the Abilities API with per-ability admin toggles (the [Enable Abilities for MCP](https://wordpress.org/plugins/enable-abilities-for-mcp/) pattern, 2,000+ installs, proves the demand), defaulting **read-only with writes opt-in per ability**. Then go past the state of the art: the incumbents' entire guardrail story is `permission_callback` plus refusal, and the category's worst incident is silent meta corruption with no undo (§4). Dry-run, a visible diff, and a restorable revision on every write is a feature Royal MCP, Easy MCP AI and WPVibe do not have — and it is precisely what makes agencies (39% already running agents, but wanting *safety* features next) willing to switch it on.

### The 3 things to deliberately NOT build

1. **Do not build another MCP server.**
   The category is saturated and free — Royal MCP (10,000+, 200+ tools), WPVibe by SeedProd (10,000+), Easy MCP AI by Themeisle (9,000+, 243 tools), miniOrange, StifLi, BetterDocs, AI Engine — all shipped by funded teams within eight months. More importantly, **the support burden is the worst in the category**: ~35 of 60 threads across the two leaders are OAuth/DCR/transport failures caused by SiteGround nginx, LiteSpeed, Cloudflare, ModSecurity and cache layers, and both vendors run pinned triage threads. Register clean Abilities instead and let the official [MCP Adapter](https://github.com/WordPress/mcp-adapter) — now being prepared for directory submission — expose them. You inherit core's OAuth work rather than repeating it.

2. **Do not build a generic front-end support / lead-gen chat widget.**
   It is the one thing an Alpaca Bot user asked for, but the expectations attached are live-agent handoff, Slack/CRM, lead capture, transcripts, analytics and per-visitor rate limiting, contested by Tidio (70,000+), Chatway (30,000+), Buttonizer (50,000+), Jotform (5,000+), WPBot and MxChat. It also imports the exact failure that produced a 1-star AI Engine review — *"all the tokens were eaten in few days by fake empty requests"* — onto a public endpoint. If the front end is served at all, serve it narrowly: a grounded, capped, read-only answer box over the site's own content, as a byproduct of build-item 3.

3. **Do not ship another Ollama connector plugin, and do not build an AI SEO/content writer.**
   The connector slot is taken *and* officially blessed — Fueled's plugin is one of four the WordPress AI team put up for community testing — and every dedicated local connector in the directory has under 300 active installs, so the slot is simultaneously occupied and empty of demand. Depend on it, don't duplicate it. (If a connector ever *is* warranted, the [Featured Connector criteria](https://github.com/WordPress/ai/blob/develop/docs/FEATURED_CONNECTORS.md) are the bar: directory-listed, tagged `ai` and `connector`, built on the AI Client SDK, core connector functionality free of any paid plugin, credentials stored securely, costs communicated up front, actively maintained — with acceptance discretionary and removable.) Separately, AI content/SEO writing is owned by incumbents with unmatchable distribution: Rank Math (4,000,000+), AIOSEO (2,000,000+), Yoast (10,000,000+), GetGenie (80,000+).

### Two risks to state plainly

- **Overlap with core.** Build-items 3, 4 and 5 partially overlap with the published **Site Agent** roadmap item and draft issue #1004. That issue is still a feature-flagged draft with unresolved UX questions, so there is a window — but Alpaca Bot 1.0 must be positioned as the *local-first, budget-enforcing, audit-logged, undoable* layer **on** the Abilities API, not as a rival chat screen. If core ships the Site Agent and Alpaca Bot is still a standalone Ollama chat window, it has nothing left.
- **Agentic AI is currently being rejected as half-baked.** Angie's 3.2/5 and *"not yet ready for production"* reviews are the market telling you that shipping an agent that *mostly* works is worse than shipping a narrow one that always does. Ship read-only and grounded first; earn writes.
